IKEA Case Study 
File:Ikea logo.svg - Wikimedia Commons

Background 

IKEA entered India with stores in Hyderabad, Mumbai, and Bengaluru targeting urban middle-class buyers. First-purchase numbers looked strong. Repeat purchases didn't follow. 

Urban customers buy once and disappear. Management has asked you to investigate why and identify the real opportunity spaces before any solution is proposed. 

What the Data Shows 

58% of urban first-time buyers don't return within 12 months 
Post-purchase support tickets (assembly issues, missing parts, delivery delays) up 24% YoY 
Exit surveys flag IKEA as "too far, too overwhelming, too effortful" and this feedback has never been formally analyzed 
The digital team tracks sessions and the store team tracks footfall with no shared retention metric between them 
Competitors like Pepperfry and Urban Ladder are picking up customers after their first IKEA visit 
Nobody agrees on what's wrong. No structured research has been done. 

Your Task 

Before any solution is proposed, your job is to understand the problem. 

Start by identifying the real opportunity spaces hiding inside this data. Not symptoms, but the deeper unmet needs. Then decide which one deserves attention first and justify your choice using a prioritization framework. 

To investigate that opportunity, design a small but purposeful research plan. Decide what kind of research fits this situation and why. Write the core questions you would ask customers in a one-on-one conversation covering their experience from first purchase to why they never came back. 

The research team has proposed interviewing only customers who left positive reviews. Call out what is wrong with this approach and suggest how you would fix it.
